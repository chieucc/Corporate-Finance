# FPT Corporation — Accounting & Performance Ratios Analysis

**Company:** FPT Corporation (FPT · HoSE)
**Fiscal Period:** FY2025 (year ended 31 December 2025), with FY2024 as prior-year comparative
**Reporting Standard:** Vietnamese Accounting Standards (VAS) · Currency: Billions VND (B VND)
**Author:** Chieu Chu
**Date:** 2026-05-24
**Course:** BUS-629 International Corporate Finance, Vietnam EMBA, Shidler College of Business — University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa

---

## Executive Summary

FPT Corporation delivered an exceptional FY2025 performance across all five analytical dimensions. The company generated an Economic Value Added of 8,611.549 B VND and a Market Value Added of 119,788.643 B VND, confirming that FPT creates genuine economic value well above its 9% cost of capital. Profitability is the dominant strength: ROE of 31.44% (start equity) nearly doubles the 15–25% Southeast Asian tech-telecom benchmark, driven primarily by an operating profit margin of 16.93%. Efficiency is adequate, though receivables growth (+27% YoY) outpacing revenue growth warrants active management. Leverage is conservatively structured — long-term debt accounts for only 4.17% of total capitalization — but a 33% surge in short-term debt introduces refinancing risk that must be monitored. Liquidity is sufficient but not comfortable, with a current ratio of 1.40x providing a moderate buffer against the large current-liability base. FPT enters FY2026 from a position of financial strength, with margin protection and receivables discipline as the two highest-priority operational levers.

## Performance

FPT's performance ratios confirm a company whose market value substantially exceeds the sum of its accounting assets. Market Value Added (MVA) — the premium the equity market places over book equity — stands at **119,788.643 B VND**, implying that investors value FPT's future economic earnings stream at 3.74 times its reported net assets (Market-to-Book: **3.74x**). This multiple is consistent with a technology and services conglomerate whose principal assets are intangible: human capital, brand, and recurring software and education contracts.

The Economic Value Added (EVA) of **8,611.549 B VND** is the cleaner measure of value creation because it accounts for the cost of the capital deployed. EVA = after-tax operating income (11,872.049 B VND) minus the capital charge (9% × 36,228.896 B VND start-of-year capitalization = 3,260.601 B VND). A positive EVA of this magnitude — representing a 23.8% spread between actual returns and the cost hurdle — demonstrates that FPT's operations generate returns well above what providers of capital require. This performance is sustained by the profitability and efficiency characteristics examined in the following sections: high operating margins reduce the capital charge denominator, and the company's near-absence of long-term debt (LT debt ratio: 4.17%) keeps the capitalization base lean, amplifying EVA. Any deterioration in operating margin or an unproductive acceleration in invested capital would compress EVA disproportionately, given the current high-return starting point.

## Profitability

FPT's profitability ratios demonstrate returns that are exceptional relative to regional technology sector norms.

**Return on Assets (ROA)** measures the after-tax operating return earned per unit of assets deployed. On a start-of-year basis, ROA is **16.49%** (after-tax operating income of 11,872.049 B VND ÷ prior-year total assets of 71,999.996 B VND); on an average-assets basis, ROA is **14.83%**, reflecting the rapid asset growth during FY2025 (+22% YoY). The start-of-year basis is the more appropriate measure, as it captures the return earned on the asset base that was actually available at the beginning of the period.

**Return on Capital (ROC)** measures efficiency relative to the permanent capital base (long-term debt + equity). ROC of **32.77%** (start capital) and **29.00%** (average capital) reflects FPT's light reliance on long-term debt, which keeps the denominator small and the ratio high. These figures confirm that FPT's operating engine generates extraordinary returns on its invested capital.

**Return on Equity (ROE)** of **31.44%** (start equity) and **28.27%** (average equity) substantially exceeds the 15–25% regional tech-telecom benchmark. This positions FPT among the highest-returning public technology companies in Southeast Asia. The gap between the two ROE figures (3.17 pp) reflects the 22.5% growth in equity during FY2025, partly attributable to retained earnings and partly to capital raising (common stock grew from 24,699.460 to 29,446.001 B VND).

The margin analysis reveals a structurally efficient income statement. Net profit margin of **16.02%** and operating profit margin of **16.93%** are separated by only 0.91 percentage points — a narrow spread that reflects low interest burden (interest expense of 809.760 B VND against EBIT of 8,074.365 B VND). FPT converts a high proportion of operating income into shareholder return with minimal attrition from financing costs.

## Efficiency

FPT's efficiency ratios reflect a business model that is asset-light relative to its revenue base but faces a growing receivables management challenge.

**Asset Turnover** of **0.974x** (net sales of 70,112.825 B VND ÷ prior-year total assets of 71,999.996 B VND) sits within the 0.8–1.2x range considered typical for technology-services conglomerates. It reflects FPT's mix of capital-intensive telecommunications infrastructure and asset-light software and education segments. The ratio is approaching the lower bound of the range as the asset base expands, suggesting that FPT must sustain or accelerate revenue growth to prevent turnover compression in future periods.

**Receivables Turnover** of **6.160x** implies an **Average Collection Period (ACP) of 59.3 days** — meaning FPT's customers take nearly two months on average to settle invoices. More concerning is the trajectory: receivables grew from 11,381.524 B VND (FY2024) to 14,402.017 B VND (FY2025), a 27% increase, while net sales grew by approximately 17% over the same period. Receivables are growing faster than revenue, indicating either a lengthening of payment terms granted to customers, deterioration in collections, or both. If uncorrected, this will reduce free cash flow and pressure the liquidity ratios discussed below.

**Inventory Turnover** of **23.82x** (Days in Inventory: **15.3 days**) is consistent with FPT's predominantly service-based revenue mix. Physical inventory is minimal relative to the cost-of-goods-sold base, and there is no evidence of inventory accumulation risk. This ratio requires no further corrective attention and will not be a driver of future ratio movement unless FPT materially increases its hardware or product distribution exposure.

The margin-turnover interaction is the central efficiency narrative: FPT's ROA (16.49%) is the product of its operating margin (16.93%) and asset turnover (0.974x), as the Du Pont decomposition confirms. Improving either component strengthens ROA; the receivables drag represents the primary addressable efficiency gap.

## Leverage

FPT's leverage profile is distinguished by a structural split between low long-term financial leverage and significant short-term and operational liabilities.

**Long-term leverage** is minimal. The Long-term Debt Ratio of **4.17%** — computed as long-term debt (1,903.790 B VND) divided by total long-term capitalization (45,651.831 B VND) — signals that FPT finances its long-term asset base almost entirely with equity. The Long-term Debt-to-Equity ratio of **0.044x** reinforces this: for every 1,000 VND of equity, FPT carries only 44 VND of long-term debt. This is an unusually conservative capital structure for a company of FPT's profitability and growth profile, and it implies significant untapped debt capacity that could be deployed to accelerate investment without materially increasing financial risk.

**Total leverage** tells a more complex story. The Total Debt Ratio of **50.37%** — total liabilities (44,393.950 B VND) as a share of total assets (88,141.990 B VND) — reflects that half of FPT's asset base is financed by creditors. This arises not from long-term debt but from short-term obligations: current liabilities of 41,524.928 B VND include short-term debt of 19,169.697 B VND (up 33% from 14,445.809 B VND in FY2024) and other current liabilities of 18,518.430 B VND. The rapid accumulation of short-term debt introduces refinancing risk: if credit market conditions tighten or FPT's operating cash flow is disrupted, the rollover of this debt becomes a liquidity event.

**Debt-service capacity** is robust. Times Interest Earned of **9.97x** (EBIT of 8,074.365 B VND ÷ interest expense of 809.760 B VND) and Cash Coverage Ratio of **13.57x** — which adds back depreciation (2,914.178 B VND) to EBIT — confirm that FPT's earnings cover its interest obligations by a wide margin under both accrual and cash-flow measures. The Debt Burden ratio of **0.9461x** indicates that net income represents 94.61% of after-tax operating income, with the 5.39% gap attributable to the net cost of non-operating items and taxes beyond the statutory rate. This is structurally healthy and consistent with a company whose interest burden is modest relative to earnings.

## Liquidity

FPT's liquidity position is adequate for near-term obligations, though the buffer is moderate rather than comfortable given the scale of current liabilities.

The **Current Ratio** of **1.40x** — current assets of 58,137.438 B VND divided by current liabilities of 41,524.928 B VND — exceeds the minimum acceptable threshold of 1.0x and indicates that FPT can cover all current obligations from current assets. However, the 1.40x reading falls below the 2.0x level often cited as standard for technology companies, and the absolute scale of the current-liability base (41.5 B VND) means that even a moderate disruption in cash generation would compress this ratio quickly.

The **Quick Ratio** of **1.31x** — (cash and marketable securities of 40,153.092 B VND + receivables of 14,402.017 B VND) ÷ current liabilities of 41,524.928 B VND — confirms that FPT can meet substantially all current obligations without liquidating inventory. The difference between the current ratio (1.40x) and the quick ratio (1.31x) is only 0.09x, reflecting the fact that inventories (2,193.770 B VND) are a small component of current assets.

The **Cash Ratio** of **0.967x** — cash and marketable securities alone divided by current liabilities — is notable: FPT holds enough cash and short-term investments to cover approximately 97% of all current liabilities immediately. This is an unusually high cash position for a technology conglomerate, reflecting FPT's strong operating cash generation (10,136.043 B VND in FY2025) and its practice of maintaining a substantial liquidity reserve. The corollary, however, is that a significant portion of the balance sheet is held in low-yield cash assets rather than deployed in higher-return investments, a capital efficiency question that management should address.

## Du Pont Decomposition

The Du Pont framework decomposes FPT's Return on Equity into four multiplicative components, isolating the contribution of each operational and financial driver:

```
ROE = Leverage × Asset Turnover × Operating Profit Margin × Debt Burden
    =   2.015  ×     0.974      ×        16.93%           ×   0.9461
    = 31.43%
```

The four-component Du Pont ROE of 31.43% aligns within 0.01 percentage points of the direct ROE calculation (31.44%), confirming internal consistency (see Validation Rule 4 in the spec).

**Operating Profit Margin (16.93%)** is the primary driver of FPT's ROE. In the Du Pont framework, margin amplifies the effect of every other component: a 1 percentage-point improvement in margin raises ROE by approximately 1.84 pp (holding turnover, leverage, and debt burden constant). FPT's margin advantage over pure-play telecom peers (typical EBIT margins of 10–15%) reflects the higher-value-added nature of its software and education segments, which carry structurally superior margins relative to infrastructure-heavy telecommunications.

**Leverage (2.015x)** contributes a moderate ROE amplification. This component — total assets divided by equity — reflects the 50.37% total debt ratio: FPT uses 2.015 units of assets for every 1 unit of equity. Because long-term financial leverage is minimal, this ratio is driven primarily by current-period operational liabilities (payables, short-term debt), not by a deliberate financial leverage strategy. Increasing intentional long-term leverage could further boost ROE, but at the cost of higher financial risk.

**Asset Turnover (0.974x)** contributes less than 1:1 on assets relative to revenue, consistent with a company whose revenue base has not yet fully absorbed recent asset additions. This component is the most addressable in the medium term: if FPT's revenue growth from its FY2025 investments (net investing outflows of 11,624.743 B VND) begins to materialize, asset turnover will recover and exert a compounding upward effect on ROE.

**Debt Burden (0.9461x)** is the smallest drag on ROE. A value below 1.0 indicates that interest and tax costs reduce the after-tax operating income available to equity holders by 5.39%. Given FPT's comfortable interest coverage (TIE: 9.97x), this component is structurally stable and unlikely to deteriorate materially absent a large increase in leverage.

**Sustainability assessment:** FPT's ROE is primarily margin-sustained and not leverage-inflated — a structurally sound configuration. The key risk to ROE sustainability is margin compression from competitive pressure in the technology services market. The 33% YoY increase in short-term debt introduces a secondary risk: if this debt converts to higher-cost long-term financing, interest expense would rise, compressing the Debt Burden component.

**Time-mismatch note:** The Du Pont leverage ratio uses current year-end balances (FY2025 total assets ÷ FY2025 equity), while asset turnover uses prior-year-end assets (FY2024) as the denominator. This is a structural feature of the template: opening-period assets are the correct denominator for measuring the return earned during the period. In FY2025, the mismatch produces a negligible 0.01 pp gap between Du Pont ROE and direct ROE. In periods of rapid asset growth, this gap will widen and must be explained as an artefact of measurement timing, not an error in either formula.

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Implement an Accounts Receivable Acceleration Programme (Efficiency → Liquidity)

**Motivating finding:** Average Collection Period of 59.3 days, with receivables growing 27% YoY (11,381.524 → 14,402.017 B VND) while revenue grew approximately 17%. Receivables are expanding faster than the revenue base, signalling a structural deterioration in collection efficiency rather than simple volume growth.

**Recommended action:** Within 12 months, FPT should introduce dynamic discounting for early payment (e.g., 1.5–2% discount for payment within 30 days) for the top 20% of enterprise customers by receivables balance, and implement automated invoice escalation for invoices exceeding 45 days. For the technology services segment specifically, contract terms should be renegotiated toward milestone-based billing rather than project-completion billing.

**Expected financial impact:** Reducing ACP from 59.3 days to 45 days would free approximately 2,700 B VND in working capital (14 days × 192.090 B VND daily sales). This would improve the Quick Ratio from 1.31x toward 1.38x, reduce reliance on short-term debt to fund the working capital gap, and improve free cash flow by a similar magnitude.

---

### 2. Refinance Short-term Debt into Long-term Instruments (Leverage → Liquidity)

**Motivating finding:** Short-term debt surged 33% YoY (14,445.809 → 19,169.697 B VND), while long-term debt stands at only 1,903.790 B VND — an unusually skewed maturity profile for a company of FPT's size. Times interest earned of 9.97x and a credit profile supported by 31.44% ROE indicate that FPT has the creditworthiness to access long-term capital markets on favourable terms.

**Recommended action:** FPT should execute a liability management exercise within 18 months, refinancing 8,000–10,000 B VND of short-term revolving debt into 5–7 year fixed-rate bonds or term loans. At FPT's current credit profile, a coupon of approximately 7–8% (below the 9% WACC) would be value-accretive. This would raise the Long-term Debt Ratio from 4.17% toward 15–18%, within a prudent range for the sector.

**Expected financial impact:** Extending the maturity profile eliminates near-term rollover risk, reduces current liabilities by 8,000–10,000 B VND, and improves the Current Ratio from 1.40x toward 1.60–1.65x. The marginal increase in annual interest expense (estimated 560–800 B VND) would reduce TIE modestly from 9.97x to approximately 8.0–8.5x — still well within safe coverage territory.

---

### 3. Defend Operating Profit Margin Through Pricing Power and Cost Discipline (Profitability → Du Pont)

**Motivating finding:** Operating Profit Margin of 16.93% is the single largest contributor to FPT's 31.43% Du Pont ROE. The margin represents a 6–7 pp premium over typical Southeast Asian telecom peers (10–15% EBIT margins), sustained by FPT's software and education segments. Cost of goods sold at 63.08% of net sales and SGA at 21.25% of net sales leave limited room for further cost reduction without strategic trade-offs.

**Recommended action:** FPT should formalise a margin-floor policy that treats 16% operating margin as a minimum acceptable threshold for new contract bids, particularly in the government IT and systems integration segments where margin erosion under competitive tendering is most common. Simultaneously, the education segment — which carries structurally superior margins — should receive priority capital allocation in FY2026 budgeting.

**Expected financial impact:** Defending the margin floor prevents the most consequential ROE deterioration pathway: a 1 pp margin compression (to 15.93%) would reduce ROE by approximately 1.84 pp (to ~29.6%), all else equal. Margin defence directly sustains EVA, which is sensitive to margin because after-tax operating income is the numerator of the EVA calculation.

---

### 4. Deploy Excess Cash into Higher-Return Investments or Shareholder Returns (Performance → Liquidity)

**Motivating finding:** Cash and marketable securities of 40,153.092 B VND represent 45.6% of total assets, a holding far in excess of operational requirements implied by the cash ratio (0.967x). FPT's operating cash flow of 10,136.043 B VND already covers its near-term obligations; maintaining a cash balance equivalent to nearly 97% of all current liabilities is sub-optimal from a capital efficiency standpoint.

**Recommended action:** FPT should establish a formal capital allocation policy targeting a cash-to-current-liabilities ratio of 60–70% (approximately 25,000–29,000 B VND in cash). The excess 11,000–15,000 B VND should be deployed in one of three ways: (a) accelerated capex in high-return technology services or education infrastructure; (b) an open-market share buyback programme, which would increase EPS and ROE through equity base reduction; or (c) a special dividend. Given the current payout ratio of approximately 40.7% (dividends of 4,573.754 B VND ÷ net income of 11,232.339 B VND), option (b) is preferable as it avoids creating a permanent dividend expectation.

**Expected financial impact:** Deploying 12,000 B VND in excess cash into capex with an assumed return equal to the current ROC (32.77%) would generate approximately 3,933 B VND in incremental after-tax operating income over the investment horizon, directly expanding EVA. A buyback of equivalent size would reduce the equity base by approximately 27%, mechanically raising ROE from 31.44% toward 38–39%.

---

### 5. Establish a Receivables Securitisation Facility to Fund Investing Activities (Efficiency → Performance)

**Motivating finding:** FPT invested 11,624.743 B VND in FY2025 (capex: 5,097.919 B VND; other investing: 6,534.138 B VND), funded primarily by operating cash flow and short-term debt increases. The investment programme is large relative to the 10,136.043 B VND operating cash flow, creating a structural funding gap that pressures the short-term debt line. Meanwhile, the 14,402.017 B VND receivables balance represents an illiquid asset that, if monetised, could fund investment without increasing balance sheet leverage.

**Recommended action:** FPT should structure a receivables securitisation or factoring facility of 6,000–8,000 B VND backed by its enterprise and government IT receivables, targeting an advance rate of 80–85% at a cost below the company's short-term borrowing rate. This would convert the receivables drag on asset turnover into an immediate source of investment funding, simultaneously improving ACP and reducing the need for short-term debt issuance.

**Expected financial impact:** A 7,000 B VND facility would reduce net receivables from 14,402 to approximately 7,400 B VND, improving ACP from 59.3 to approximately 38.5 days. This improves the Quick Ratio, lowers short-term debt requirements by 7,000 B VND (reducing total debt ratio from 50.37% toward 42.4%), and frees the investment programme from dependence on credit market conditions.
